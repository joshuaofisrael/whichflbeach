Beaches where the park page talks about shelling

By Which FL Beach · Last verified 16 Nov 2026

How this list is made. A beach is here only when the managing agency page mentions shells, shelling, or beachcombing. Famous reputations that the park page does not state are left off.

FWC says you may collect a sea shell depending on whether anything is living in it, what that animal is, and where you are. A recreational saltwater fishing license is required to take a shell that contains a living organism, even from shore. Live oysters and live hard clams follow their own rules and may be taken only from approved harvest areas that are open. In Lee County, FWC says you may not harvest or possess a shell containing a live organism, with stated exceptions for oysters, hard clams, sunray venus clams and coquinas. In Manatee County the limit is two live shells of any single species per day, with the same exceptions, and the count includes sand dollars and starfish. Bahama starfish harvest is prohibited. Live queen conch may not be possessed. An empty queen conch shell has conditions on the FWC page, including no hole of the kind the rule describes. Many state park pages add their own ban on taking live shells. Ask the park if the page is unclear.

Beaches

  • Coquina Beach. Coquina Beach is a Manatee County beach on Anna Maria with a lifeguard station, beach wheelchairs, a playground, and an introduction that talks about the sun setting over the water.
  • Honeymoon Island State Park. Honeymoon Island is the Pinellas state park with a named pet beach, shelling on the north spit, and free beach wheelchairs.
  • Caladesi Island State Park. Caladesi is the island park reached by ferry from Honeymoon Island. The page promotes shelling and bans live shells and pets on the beach.
  • Anclote Key Preserve State Park. Anclote Key is a boat or ferry preserve in Pasco. Shells are mentioned, live shellfish are not allowed, and dogs are limited to North Anclote Bar.
  • Egmont Key State Park. Egmont Key is a boat only island in Tampa Bay with shelling, no restrooms, and a south end the page closes as a shorebird refuge.
  • Stump Pass Beach State Park. Stump Pass is the Charlotte County state park beach the page ties to winter shelling, with free wheelchairs and no lifeguards.
  • Don Pedro Island State Park. Don Pedro is a boat only Gulf beach in Charlotte County. The drive up land base is not the swim.
  • Lovers Key State Park. Lovers Key is the Lee County state park that calls its own beach some of the best shelling on that coast, and that warns the lot fills on busy weekends.
  • Gasparilla Island State Park. Gasparilla Island State Park is the Boca Grande Gulf beach where the page highlights winter shelling and several named lots.
  • Cayo Costa State Park. Cayo Costa is a boat access Lee County island the park page recommends for shells, with about nine miles of Gulf beach in the description.
  • Delnor Wiggins Pass State Park. Delnor Wiggins is the Collier state park the page credits with some of the best Gulf shelling, four beach lots, and both manual and electric beach wheelchairs.
  • Deer Lake State Park. Deer Lake is the smaller Walton state park: a half mile of beach, a boardwalk, shells mentioned, and no lifeguards.
  • Perdido Key State Park. Perdido Key State Park is the Escambia Gulf beach the page ties to winter shelling, with chairs requested through Big Lagoon.
  • St. George Island State Park. St. George Island State Park is the Franklin County Gulf park with 9 miles of beach in the description, mats at named accesses, and several kinds of beach chair.
  • Anastasia State Park. Anastasia is the St. Augustine state park with four miles of beach, a seasonal lifeguard window, a mat, and shelling the page describes.
  • North Peninsula State Park. North Peninsula is the Atlantic park north of the Flagler line in this guide, with 2.5 miles of beach, shelling, surfing, and no lifeguard.
  • Little Talbot Island State Park. Little Talbot is the Duval barrier island park with shelling, fossil shark teeth on the park's own list, two beach wheelchairs, and no lifeguards.
  • Sebastian Inlet State Park. Sebastian Inlet is the Atlantic park with a protected cove the page recommends for children, plus surf, beachcombing, and no lifeguards.
  • Fort Pierce Inlet State Park. Fort Pierce Inlet State Park is the St. Lucie beach with a firm bottom, shells, snorkeling, and a free wheelchair you request at the gate.
  • John D. MacArthur Beach State Park. MacArthur Beach is the Palm Beach County state park with nearly two miles of Atlantic beach, three free wheelchairs, and shelling in the park's own list.
  • Fort Zachary Taylor Historic State Park. Fort Zachary Taylor is the Key West state park the page calls the best swimming on the island, with snorkeling from shore and a rocky bottom.
  • Curry Hammock State Park. Curry Hammock is the Marathon state park with a short Atlantic beach, beachcombing, and a lot that closes when it is full.

Questions people ask

Can I take a live shell home?

FWC requires a recreational saltwater fishing license to take a shell with a living animal, even from shore, and some counties and parks ban or limit live shells.

Why is a famous shelling beach missing?

If the county or state park page we checked does not mention shells, it is not on this list. Tell us if a newer page does.
