Which Florida beaches have shore snorkeling?

By Which FL Beach · Last verified 19 Nov 2026

How this list is made. A beach is here only when the managing agency page describes snorkeling from shore or nearshore from that beach. Boat only reef tours do not count as shore snorkeling. We do not invent visibility, fish counts, or a safety rating.

Checked against Florida State Parks experiences pages on 19 Nov 2026. Most of the shore snorkeling the agencies describe sits in the Florida Keys, with Fort Pierce Inlet on the Atlantic and Anclote Key on the Gulf as published exceptions. Lifeguard lines below are the park wording. We never call a beach safe; read the flags and the agency page the day you go. For water quality and red tide, use our red tide guide and the FWC and Florida Department of Health links, not a saved copy of this page.

Beaches the park page ties to shore snorkeling

  • Anclote Key Preserve State Park. The park page says the clear gulf waters are an excellent spot to snorkel and observe sea life. Entry is by boat or ferry; there is no car lot on the island. Entry and parking: see the beach page. Lifeguards: The park page says lifeguards are not provided. Swim at your own risk and read the flag sign.
  • Fort Pierce Inlet State Park. The park page describes a shore accessible saltwater site with a reef about 100 yards from the beach, maximum depth about 16 to 20 feet, and says the abundance of tropical fish makes it a great spot for snorkeling or scuba. A diver down flag is required for scuba. No lifeguards are on duty. Entry and parking: see the beach page. Lifeguards: The page says to swim at your own risk and that no lifeguards are on duty.
  • Loggerhead Beach at Bahia Honda. The Bahia Honda park page says the park has some of the best nearshore snorkeling in the Florida Keys, with sea life a few hundred feet from shore in about 4 to 6 feet of water, and that it is good for beginners. A diver down flag is required for scuba. No lifeguards are provided. Loggerhead is the Atlantic facing day use shore in that park. Entry and parking: see the beach page. Lifeguards: The page says no lifeguards are provided.
  • Calusa Beach at Bahia Honda. The Bahia Honda park page says the park has some of the best nearshore snorkeling in the Florida Keys, with sea life a few hundred feet from shore in about 4 to 6 feet of water, and that it is good for beginners. A diver down flag is required for scuba. No lifeguards are provided. Calusa is the Florida Bay facing shore with the picnic pavilions in that park. Entry and parking: see the beach page. Lifeguards: The page says no lifeguards are provided.
  • Sandspur Beach at Bahia Honda. The Bahia Honda park page says the park has some of the best nearshore snorkeling in the Florida Keys, with sea life a few hundred feet from shore in about 4 to 6 feet of water, and that it is good for beginners. A diver down flag is required for scuba. No lifeguards are provided. Sandspur is the Atlantic day use shore with parking along the beach. Entry and parking: see the beach page. Lifeguards: The page says no lifeguards are provided.
  • Fort Zachary Taylor Historic State Park. The park page says Fort Zachary Taylor provides some of the best snorkeling from the beach in the Florida Keys and calls it good for beginners. The beach section describes sand and pebbles plus nearby breakwaters with tropical fish. Water shoes are recommended. Gear can be rented at the concession. No lifeguards are on duty. Entry and parking: see the beach page. Lifeguards: The page says no lifeguards are on duty.
  • Cannon Beach at John Pennekamp. The park page calls Cannon Beach the primary snorkeling beach, with artifacts from a 1715 Spanish shipwreck placed underwater about 100 feet offshore. Swimming or snorkeling outside the designated area needs a diver down flag. No lifeguards are on duty. Offshore coral reef visits are boat tours, not this beach. Entry and parking: see the beach page. Lifeguards: The page says there are no lifeguards and swimming is at your own risk.
  • Curry Hammock State Park. The park page says to snorkel the shallow seagrass flat, where marine life is abundant, and calls it a place to learn before heading to the coral reef. A diver down flag is required anywhere while snorkeling. No lifeguards are provided. Entry and parking: see the beach page. Lifeguards: The page says no lifeguards are provided.

Far Beach at John Pennekamp is in the same park as Cannon Beach. The park describes Far Beach as a swim or palm shade shore, not as the artifact snorkel beach, so it is left off this list. Offshore Pennekamp coral is reached by boat tour; that is a different product from Cannon Beach.

Compare another shore

Open Cannon Beach for the artifact snorkel, Fort Zachary Taylor for Key West breakwaters, Loggerhead Beach at Bahia Honda for the park nearshore note, or Fort Pierce Inlet for the Atlantic reef about 100 yards out.

Questions people ask

Can I snorkel the Florida reef from every Keys beach?

No. Florida State Parks pages for Pennekamp describe offshore reef visits by boat. Cannon Beach is the park's primary shore snorkel with placed shipwreck artifacts in shallow water.

Do these beaches have lifeguards?

Only if that beach page says so. The Keys state park pages we cite for shore snorkeling say lifeguards are not provided. Read the beach page.

Where should I check water conditions?

We do not post live water quality. Use FWC red tide tools and the Florida Department of Health Healthy Beaches map the morning you go.
