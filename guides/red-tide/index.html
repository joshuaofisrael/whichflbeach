Red tide history and where to check today

By Which FL Beach · Last verified 16 Nov 2026

Check today, do not trust a saved page. Open the FWC current status page and the daily sample map. Then open the Florida Department of Health Healthy Beaches map for bacteria. This guide explains the background. It does not state this week's cell counts.

What FWC says it is

FWC describes red tide as a higher than normal concentration of a microscopic alga. In Florida, the species behind most red tides is Karenia brevis. At high concentrations the water can turn red or brown, and it can also stay its normal color.

It is not new, and it is not only one coast

FWC says red tides were documented along Florida's Gulf coast in the 1840s, and that fish kills near Tampa Bay appear in the records of Spanish explorers. Blooms most often form in late summer and early fall and can persist through winter into spring. A bloom can last a few weeks or longer than a year, and it can fade and return. FWC says Karenia brevis is found almost exclusively in the Gulf, from Mexico to Florida, and that some blooms have been carried by the Gulf Stream into the Atlantic as far as Delaware. The organism can show up in bays and estuaries. It does not persist in freshwater.

What it can do

FWC says Karenia brevis produces brevetoxins that can kill fish, and that wave action can put toxins in the air and irritate breathing. People with asthma or other chronic respiratory disease can become seriously ill. FWC says not to swim in or around red tide because it can irritate skin and eyes. That is the agency's warning, not a diagnosis. For a suspected exposure, FWC points people to a doctor or Florida Poison Information. Shellfish from a restaurant or market are monitored. Recreational clams, oysters and mussels from a closed area are not a safe harvest. Cooking does not remove the toxin. Pets can be affected. FWC says not to let them eat dead fish or foam, and to rinse them if they swim in a bloom.

Where to look the day you go

  • FWC current status, updated on a schedule the status page explains, plus a phone recording the same page describes.
  • The daily sample map of the last eight days, which FWC says is updated daily.
  • Beach conditions reports from Mote Marine Laboratory, which FWC lists as a complement to its own reports.
  • The Florida Department of Health beach water map for enterococci, which is a different problem from red tide. The department describes an advisory when a sample over 70 colony forming units per 100 milliliters is confirmed by a resample.

Gulf beaches in this guide sit on the coast with the long written record. Atlantic and Keys pages explain why a clear headline for the other coast is not enough. County pages sometimes add their own red tide link. Use that too.

Questions people ask

Is the red tide bad at my beach today?

We do not answer that on this site. Open the FWC status page and the daily sample map, then the health department map.

When does red tide usually happen?

FWC says blooms most often form in late summer and early fall and can last into spring. They also occur outside that window.

Does red tide reach the Atlantic side?

FWC says some blooms have been carried by the Gulf Stream into the Atlantic, as far as Delaware.
