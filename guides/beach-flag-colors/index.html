What do Florida beach warning flags mean?

By Which FL Beach · Last verified 20 Nov 2026

Five signals, and not today's color. Florida public beaches that fly warning flags are supposed to use the set developed by the Department of Environmental Protection. The sign file names the hazards. County and park pages name the colors. This guide does not say which flag is flying this afternoon.

Checked 20 Nov 2026. Section 380.276 puts the uniform flag program under DEP, through the Florida Coastal Management Program. A public beach that displays warning flags is limited to the flags the department developed, unless the statute's later subsection allows an extra device. The program is open to any coastal government, with or without lifeguards. The DEP program page says the 2005 amendment is what required that standardization, after a 2002 law that only called for a uniform beach safety program. The same page says DEP built the flags with the Florida Beach Patrol Chiefs Association, the United States Lifesaving Association, and the International Life Saving Federation.

The sign says the hazard. The color name is on the county page.

The DEP sign file prints five rows and does not, in the text of that file, print the words green, yellow, red, or purple. The rows are: water closed to the public; high hazard, high surf and/or strong currents; medium hazard, moderate surf and/or currents; low hazard, calm conditions, exercise caution; and stinging marine life, naming man of war, jellyfish, and stingrays. The last line says a pole with no flag is not a clearance to enter.

The program page says the system uses flags in four colors. Double red is two flags of the red color, so four colors can be five signals. Counties spell the colors out:

A flag is not a rip current forecast, and it is not a lifeguard

DEP says the flags are a general warning about surf conditions. They do not specifically tell you a rip current is present. Since 2004 the coastal program has also handed out separate rip current signs developed with the National Weather Service, Sea Grant, and USLA. Read that poster if it is on the walkway. It is a different object from the flag.

USLA says its flag set is approved only on beaches where lifeguards trained to USLA standards are on duty, and that a flag is a tool for those lifeguards, not a substitute for them. Florida's statute is wider: a beach with no lifeguards can still join the flag program. A flag on an unguarded shore does not create a lifeguard. The lifeguard fact, if any, is whatever that park or county page prints. This site does not call a beach clear to enter.

The flags measure 29.25 inches high by 39 inches wide. The signs are 30 by 36 inches. DEP provides flags and signs to local governments when funds allow. The city or county that takes them has to put them up and keep them up.

Which beaches in this guide actually post them

Volusia prints the five colors and the missing flag warning. Volusia is not one of the counties in this beach guide. It is here because the color names are clearer there than on the DEP sign file.

South Walton Fire District says this is the system on the beaches of South Walton, about 26 miles, with the color chosen from the most dangerous surf on that stretch and then flown at each access. The water in front of you can look calmer than the flag. The district posts a current color on its own page. This guide does not copy that color. Our Walton pages are state parks: Grayton Beach, Topsail Hill, and Deer Lake. Those park pages do not print the five colors. Read the flag at the access, then the district page, and do not assume a state park tower and a fire district tower are the same schedule.

Fort Lauderdale Ocean Rescue describes about three miles of city beach and 20 towers, from the South Beach picnic area north to Hugh Taylor Birch State Park. The city prints spring and summer hours of 9:45 a.m. to 7 p.m., and fall and winter hours of 9:15 a.m. to 6 p.m. Naming the state park as the north end of that run is not the same thing as saying the park's own shore is on the city schedule. Use the city page for the towers and Hugh Taylor Birch for the state park.

Henderson Beach State Park was rechecked the same day. The swimming section lists double red, red, yellow, green, and purple, and tells you to read the signs at the welcome stations and on the beach. The beach section of that same page, in the copy we read, skips the red row. Use the sign at the lot, not a memory of either list. The page does not say a lifeguard is on duty. Its green line goes further than the DEP sign. We do not repeat that line.

Delnor-Wiggins uses the colors two ways. Warning flag signs are posted at each parking area entrance and on the beach, and the page says lifeguards are not provided. Separately, swimming stops at the double red flags at the northern end of parking area four because of currents in Wiggins Pass. That double red is a boundary written into the park text, not a color we update.

Anastasia tells swimmers to heed posted warnings, including rip currents, and the experiences page does not print the five colors. Lifeguards there are a summer window, Memorial Day through Labor Day, at one designated ramp. A flag, if one is flying, is still not that staffing fact.

Questions people ask

What does a double red flag mean?

The DEP sign says the water is closed to the public. Volusia and South Walton use two red flags for that row. Fort Lauderdale's page also lists pollution, lightning, and predatory fish as reasons that city may close the water. That extra list is the city's, not the statute.

Does green mean the water is clear to enter?

No. The DEP low hazard row says calm conditions, exercise caution, and the sign says a missing flag is not a clearance. USLA's international set does not include green. Do not treat a green flag as permission.

Does purple mean sharks?

USLA says purple is for marine pests such as jellyfish or stingrays and is not meant to indicate sharks. The DEP sign names man of war, jellyfish, and stingrays.

Are the flags a rip current warning?

DEP says no. They are a general surf warning. Rip current signs are a separate poster.
