Which Florida beaches have free parking?

By Which FL Beach · Last verified 21 Nov 2026

In this guide, free parking is a Sarasota County sentence. The state park beaches we opened on 21 Nov 2026 charge a vehicle admission, and the printed amount is not the same park to park. Pinellas prints a county fee or says hourly and daily rates apply. A beach with no car lot is not a free lot.

A row is here only when we opened that agency page on 21 Nov 2026 and it said free, printed a price, or said there is no lot. If a page is silent, we do not call the lot free. None of these pages used the word meter. A flat fee, a pair of hourly and daily rates, and a vehicle admission are three different things, and this page keeps them apart.

Sarasota is the free column, with named holes

The beaches directory says parking is available at select beaches and parks for free on a first-come, first-served basis. Siesta Beach is the page that counts the spaces: over 950, and the hours page says that main lot is free from 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. year round. The same hours page says there is no overnight parking in county beach lots.

The directory titles that remove the lot are North Lido, Palmer Point (watercraft access only), Siesta accesses 3, 3B, 10, 11, and 13, and Turtle Beach Campground. Access 2 is titled as one accessible space and no other parking. This guide does not give Access 2 its own page. Access 11 is the odd title: no parking, and a lifeguard still on the amenity list. The hours page adds that accesses 3, 3B, 10, 11, and 13 stay open 24 hours and still have no parking.

Caspersen is not in the free column. The facility page checked 20 Nov 2026 said the vehicle lot was closed. We did not reopen that facility page today. A closed lot is not a lot that costs nothing.

State parks do not share one price

The statewide fees page says daily entrance is per vehicle for two to eight people, that county surcharges may apply, and that children under six are admitted free. It tells you to open the park page for the number. We did. Anastasia is 8 dollars for a vehicle with up to eight people. Henderson Beach and Big Lagoon are 6 dollars on that same split, and Big Lagoon adds a separate boat launch price. Big Talbot is the cheap outlier at 3 dollars, except the wayside picnic area, which the picnicking section says has no entry fee. Copying one of those numbers onto the next state park would be a guess. Most experiences pages in this guide still do not print the day use fee at all. Those parks are not listed above, and they are not free by omission.

Pinellas prints a county charge

Fort De Soto is a flat 6 dollars, payable by app, text, terminal, or an annual pass, with a 0.35 dollar convenience fee on the app and on text. Sand Key says hourly and daily rates apply and does not print either number. We leave the blank. Both pages were opened 21 Nov 2026. Hours on both are 7 a.m. to sunset.

No car lot

Anclote Key is the clearest case we rechecked. The experiences page says boat or ferry from Tarpon Springs or New Port Richey, and it stops there. No operator, no fare, no island lot. Pasco County in this guide is that one preserve. Treat a ferry you have not priced as an unknown, not as free parking.

Questions people ask

Which beaches in this guide have free parking?

Sarasota County's directory, checked 21 Nov 2026, says parking at select beaches and parks is free and first come, first served. Siesta Beach states over 950 free spaces. A directory title that says no parking is not a free lot.

Do Florida State Parks beaches include free parking?

Not on the pages we opened 21 Nov 2026. The statewide fees page charges a vehicle admission and does not print one dollar amount. Anastasia lists 8 dollars. Henderson Beach and Big Lagoon list 6 dollars. Big Talbot lists 3 dollars, and its wayside picnic area has no entry fee.

Is a beach free to park if the page never names a price?

No. Silence is not a free lot. Sand Key says hourly and daily rates apply and does not print the amount. We do not fill that gap.

Are the lots metered?

The county and state park pages checked for this guide do not describe meters. They describe a free county lot, a flat county fee, hourly and daily rates, or a vehicle admission.
